# Designer Brief — BLTZ Player Locker

## The one-liner
Design the page that becomes an athlete's entire career, gathered into one permanent, player-owned home — a single link that is the definitive place to showcase who they are as a player.

## The mission
Today an athlete's career is scattered — highlight films on one site, stats on another, news articles somewhere else, recognition buried in social media. The Locker pulls all of it into one place the athlete owns and controls. It's built to be *sent*: to recruiters, coaches, scouts, media, sponsors, and fans. The whole job of the page is to make someone who lands on it instantly understand, believe in, and remember this player. One link tells the whole story.

## Who it's for (the people landing on it)
- **Recruiters, coaches, and scouts** evaluating the player — they want proof, fast.
- **Media and sponsors** sizing up the athlete's story and reputation.
- **Fans, teammates, and community** who want to follow and champion them.
- **The athlete themselves**, who should feel proud to share it — it represents them.

## The feeling it should create
Pride, credibility, and permanence. It should feel like *the athlete's own*, not a generic profile in a database. Someone should leave the page convinced this player is the real thing and that their career has been treated with respect.

## What each element needs to accomplish
Design each piece around the job it does for the athlete — not just the information it holds:

- **Identity** (name, position, school, jersey number, years active, hometown) — answer "who is this and where do they come from" in a glance, and anchor everything else.
- **Headshot** — make it personal and real; put a face to the name.
- **Tagline** — land one line that captures what defines this athlete.
- **Claim This Locker** — communicate that this page was built *for* the athlete and is *theirs* to own and control.
- **Share & QR** — make the page feel built to travel; sending it should be effortless and obvious.
- **Highlight videos** — the single most important proof. The page should make watching the player perform feel inevitable.
- **Photo gallery** — carry the emotion and moments of the career, not just the data.
- **College career** — tell the foundational chapter: signature stats, peak season, honors.
- **Pro career** — establish the highest-level chapter: seasons, teams, draft pedigree, career totals, accolades.
- **Full stats** — satisfy the detail-seekers with complete season-by-season numbers and honors.
- **In The Press** — deliver third-party validation: the player's story and reputation as told by others.
- **Believers** — social proof from teammates and followers who vouch for the player; credibility and community.
- **Search** — connect this locker into the wider network of athletes, schools, and teams.

## Content priority (most important to the athlete's story to least)
1. Who they are (identity + face).
2. Proof on film (highlights).
3. The career chapters and their defining numbers / honors.
4. Outside validation (press + believers).
5. Ownership and sharing (claim, share).

Lead with what convinces and what's most personal; let supporting evidence build behind it.

**Awards note:** automated scraping returns only a few honors per athlete. The page should look complete and intentional even when an athlete has a short awards list — design for the sparse case, not just the full one.

## Success criteria
- A first-time visitor "gets" the player within seconds and wants to keep going.
- The page feels athlete-owned and premium — something the player is proud to share.
- It works as a sending tool: the link and code make it spread naturally.
- Every element earns its place by advancing the athlete's story; nothing feels like filler.

## Tone & voice
Confident, plain-spoken, and earned. Let the film, the numbers, and the recognition do the talking — no hype, no clichés.
